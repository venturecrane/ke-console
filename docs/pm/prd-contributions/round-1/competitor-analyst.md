## 1. Competitive Landscape

### Market Overview

The co-parenting app market is experiencing significant growth, valued at **$440 million in 2024** and projected to reach **$1.12 billion by 2032**, representing a **12.4% CAGR**. The fastest-growing segment is dual-household co-parenting users, with an estimated growth rate of 15% per annum, driven by rising demand for shared-custody coordination and expense management.

### Market Context

- **13 million custodial parents** in the United States (approximately 4% of total population)
- **22 million children** under 21 have a parent living outside their household
- Only **11% of custody cases** result in an agreement for both parents to share custody equally
- **$110+ billion** in unpaid child support in the US, indicating significant pain around financial coordination
- Average custodial parent receives **$3,431/year** in child support, but only two-thirds receive full amounts owed

### Key Market Trends

1. **Court Integration** - Apps increasingly marketed as "court-admissible" or "court-ordered," with OurFamilyWizard being mandated by judges in high-conflict cases
2. **AI-Powered Communication** - Newer entrants (BestInterest) using AI for tone filtering and conflict reduction
3. **Consolidation** - OurFamilyWizard acquired Cozi in 2022, signaling market consolidation
4. **Mobile-First** - Most apps now prioritize mobile experience, though many still require native app downloads
5. **Payment Integration** - Growing expectation of in-app payments (OFWpay, DComply, Onward ACH)

### Competitive Categories

| Category                    | Players                                            | Focus                                    |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **Full-Suite Co-Parenting** | OurFamilyWizard, TalkingParents, AppClose, 2Houses | Calendar + Messaging + Expenses          |
| **Expense-Focused**         | DComply, SupportPay, Onward (defunct)              | Payments and expense tracking            |
| **Communication-Focused**   | TalkingParents, BestInterest                       | Documented messaging, conflict reduction |
| **Free/Freemium**           | Cent, BestInterest (free tier)                     | Market entry, upsell                     |
| **DIY/Spreadsheets**        | Splitwise, Google Sheets                           | Low-conflict, cost-conscious             |

## 2. Competitor Profiles

### OurFamilyWizard

**The Incumbent Leader**

| Attribute           | Details                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Positioning**     | "The Best Co-Parenting App for Child Custody" - Court-recognized standard                                                                |
| **Target Market**   | High-conflict divorced parents, court-ordered users                                                                                      |
| **Key Features**    | Shared calendar, messaging with ToneMeter AI, expense log with OFWpay, document storage, journal, visitation tracker                     |
| **Pricing**         | Essentials: $12.50/mo, Premium: $18/mo, Max: $24.99/mo (per parent)                                                                      |
| **Expense Dispute** | Binary approve/refuse system; refused expenses excluded from totals                                                                      |
| **Split Options**   | 50/50 or 80/20 or 100% - limited flexibility                                                                                             |
| **Strengths**       | Court recognition in all 50 states, longest track record, comprehensive feature set, professional network (lawyers, mediators)           |
| **Weaknesses**      | Expensive ($216-600/year for family), mixed reviews (1.5 stars SiteJabber, 2.1 stars Google), rigid split options, both parents must pay |
| **User Complaints** | Drafts don't save, search function unreliable, calendar "viewed" status gameable, Face ID issues, expense splits limited                 |

**Key Insight:** OFW owns the "court-ordered" segment but has significant UX debt and pricing that creates barriers for amicable co-parents.

| **Cent**            | Yes (full)    | -          | -        | -                  |

### Pricing Analysis

**KE at $5/mo is competitively positioned:**

1. **Below OFW** ($12.50-25) - The market leader charges 2.5-5x more
2. **At DComply Premium** ($5.99) - Closest expense-focused competitor
3. **Below AppClose** ($8.99) - The rising "all-in-one" threat
4. **Below 2Houses** ($7) - International competitor with similar features
5. **Above DComply Basic** ($2.99) - But KE offers more features
6. **Competes with Cent** - Must win on features since Cent is free

**Key Pricing Insights:**

- **$5/mo is in the "sweet spot"** - Below full-suite apps, at parity with expense-focused apps
- **Free tier is essential** - Cent, TalkingParents, BestInterest all have meaningful free tiers
- **Both-parent pricing matters** - 2Houses and BestInterest Family include co-parent, reducing adoption friction
- **Annual discounts common** - Most apps offer 15-20% savings for annual billing

**Recommendation:** $5/mo is defensible. Consider a "Family" plan ($8/mo) that includes the co-parent to reduce adoption friction.

## 5. Differentiation Analysis

### KE's Genuine Differentiators

| Differentiator                         | Why It Matters                                                                                                                               | Competitor Gap                                                    |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **8-Status Dispute Workflow**          | Real disputes have nuance beyond "approved/refused." Questioning, escalation, timeout, and resolution states model real-world back-and-forth | OFW only has binary approve/refuse; others even less              |
| **Balance-Excluded Questioning**       | Disputed items shouldn't count toward who owes whom until resolved. This prevents one parent gaming the balance                              | No competitor explicitly offers this                              |
| **Rolling Balance with Carry Forward** | Monthly resets lose context; rolling balance shows true picture                                                                              | Some have balance, but carry-forward behavior unclear             |
| **Full Audit Trail (Before/After)**    | Every change logged with what was changed, by whom. True accountability                                                                      | OFW has "unalterable" messages but expense audit trail less clear |
| **Mobile-Web-First**                   | No app store friction. Works on any device immediately                                                                                       | Most competitors require native app downloads                     |
| **Simple, Focused UX**                 | Expenses only, not a full co-parenting suite                                                                                                 | Competitors bundle everything, creating complexity                |

### Table Stakes (Must Have)

- Shared expense ledger
- Receipt attachments (roadmap)
- Expense categories
- Split ratio configuration
- Recurring expenses
- Export capability (Pro)

### Competitive Advantages

- **Dispute workflow depth** - The 8-status system with 7-day timeout and 30-day close is genuinely novel
- **Balance integrity** - Excluding questioned items from balance calculations is smart design
- **Audit trail completeness** - Before/after snapshots provide true legal-grade documentation
- **Web-first simplicity** - No app download barrier; works immediately on any device

### Potential Weaknesses

- **No calendar** - Full-suite apps bundle calendar, making KE feel incomplete
- **No messaging** - Same bundling pressure
- **No in-app payments** - OFWpay, ipayou, DComply payments are increasingly expected
- **Newer entrant** - No court recognition, no professional network

## 6. Competitive Risks

### Risk 1: OurFamilyWizard Improves Expense Disputes

**Threat Level:** Medium
**Scenario:** OFW notices users wanting more nuanced dispute workflows and ships an 8-status system
**Mitigation:** Move fast. KE's 8-status workflow should be deeply integrated with balance calculations, audit trails, and export. Make it hard to copy superficially.

### Risk 2: DComply Expands to Full Suite

**Threat Level:** Medium-High
**Scenario:** DComply is the closest competitor in expense-focus. If they add calendar/messaging, they become direct competition at a lower price point ($5.99 vs $5)
**Mitigation:** Differentiate on workflow sophistication (8 statuses), balance integrity, and audit trails. DComply's "Venmo for co-parents" positioning is payment-centric, not dispute-centric.

### Risk 3: Free Competitors (Cent) Capture the Market

**Threat Level:** Medium
**Scenario:** Cent is free and recommended by divorce financial advisors. If they refine their product, KE's $5/mo is a hard sell
**Mitigation:** KE's free tier must be genuinely useful. 3-month history, 2 recurring expenses is reasonable. Win on dispute workflow and balance integrity features that Cent lacks.

### Risk 4: AI-Native Entrants (BestInterest) Add Expenses

**Threat Level:** Low-Medium
**Scenario:** BestInterest has strong AI positioning and recent launch. If they add robust expense features, they could capture modern-minded users
**Mitigation:** BestInterest is communication-focused. Expense tracking is not trivial to do well. KE should focus on being the best at expenses rather than adding AI.

### Risk 5: Well-Funded Competitor Enters

**Threat Level:** Medium-High
**Scenario:** A well-funded startup or incumbent (Intuit, PayPal, banks) sees the $1B market opportunity and launches with marketing budget
**Mitigation:** The co-parenting market is relationship-sensitive (users don't switch easily once set up). Move fast to capture users. Focus on word-of-mouth through family lawyers and mediators.

### Risk 6: App Store Distribution Advantage

**Threat Level:** Medium
**Scenario:** Native app competitors have App Store discoverability. Web apps rely on direct marketing
**Mitigation:** SEO, content marketing (free co-parenting expense calculators, templates), professional referral network. Mobile-web-first is also an advantage (no download friction).

## 7. Positioning Statement

### Current Positioning (from PRD)

> "A neutral ledger for kid costs - so money doesn't become a weekly argument."

### Refined Positioning

**For:** Divorced and separated parents who share child expenses
**Who:** Need clear, accountable records without the complexity and cost of full co-parenting suites
**KE is:** A focused expense tracking app with sophisticated dispute resolution
**That:** Eliminates financial arguments through transparent ledger, smart dispute workflow, and true accountability
**Unlike:** OurFamilyWizard and other full-suite apps that bundle expensive features you don't need, or simple trackers that don't handle real-world disputes
**KE:** Does expenses right - with the nuanced 8-status workflow, balance-protected questioning, and complete audit trail that high-conflict situations actually require
