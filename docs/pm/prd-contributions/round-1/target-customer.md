# Target Customer Feedback: Kid Expenses

**Profile:** Sarah, 38, divorced 2 years, two kids (8 and 11), 50/50 custody, currently using Google Sheets

---

## 1. First Impressions

**What excites me:**
The rolling balance is HUGE. I'm so tired of doing mental math every time we settle up. Right now I have to scroll through our Google Sheet (which my ex hasn't updated in 3 weeks), add up everything, cross-reference Venmo, and usually we end up in an argument about whether he paid me back for soccer camp or not. Having one number that just tells me "you owe him $247" or "he owes you $183" would eliminate 90% of our money fights.

The payment recording feature is also really appealing. Right now when my ex Venmos me, I have no idea what it's for. He'll send $150 with a soccer ball emoji and I'm like... is that for last month's cleats or this month's league fees? Being able to link a payment to specific expenses sounds like a game-changer.

**What confuses me:**
The 8-status workflow sounds complicated. I read through it twice and I'm still not 100% clear on when I'd use "questioned" vs just texting him "hey did you really spend $200 on new shoes?" The 7-day auto-resolution thing makes me nervous — what if I'm on vacation and miss it?

Also, what's the difference between "activities" and "childcare"? Our kids do after-school care that's basically activities. Does that matter?

**What would make me download this:**
Honestly? If my lawyer or mediator recommended it. Or if another divorced parent I trust told me it saved them from fights. I'm not randomly downloading finance apps — there's too much emotional baggage. I'd need to know this is legit and that other people in my situation actually use it.

The other thing: if it took me less than 5 minutes to set up and see real value. If I have to watch tutorials or read documentation, I'm out.

## 2. Pain Point Validation

**Problems this solves (for me):**

1. ✅ **The "who owes who" calculation** — This is 80% of our arguments. The rolling balance would eliminate this entirely.
2. ✅ **Payment disconnect** — Linking Venmo payments to specific expenses would be amazing. Right now it's total chaos.
3. ✅ **Audit trail** — When my ex claims he "already paid for that," having a timestamped log would shut that down immediately.
4. ✅ **Recurring expenses** — We have like 8 things that repeat every month (lunch accounts, music lessons, etc.). Templates would save me from logging the same thing over and over.

**Problems this misses:**

1. ❌ **Trust issues** — The real problem isn't tracking expenses, it's that I don't trust my ex to log things honestly. Can he edit expenses after I've approved them? Can he delete things retroactively? The app doesn't address the fundamental trust problem.
2. ❌ **Reimbursement timing** — We're supposed to settle up monthly, but he's always late. Does this app have any enforcement mechanism? Reminders? Anything?
3. ❌ **The "it's not in the budget" fight** — Sometimes he logs an expense I think is ridiculous (like $300 for a trampoline). The questioning workflow might help, but it doesn't solve the core issue: we never agreed on what's shareable vs personal in the first place.
4. ❌ **Integration with custody schedule** — I'd love to see which parent had the kids when an expense happened. Right now if he logs a $60 dinner expense, I have to remember "wait, did he have the kids that night or did he just take them out on my custody day?"

## 3. Feature Priority (from my perspective)

**Features I'd use constantly:**

1. **Rolling balance** — Every single day. This is the killer feature.
2. **Payment recording** — Every time we settle up (monthly).
3. **Adding expenses** — Multiple times per week.
4. **Recurring expense templates** — Would set up once and use forever.

**Features I'd use occasionally:** 5. **Activity audit trail** — Only when we're arguing about something. 6. **Questioning workflow** — Maybe once a month when he logs something suspicious. 7. **Categories** — I'd probably use them but wouldn't change my behavior if they disappeared.

**Features I'd probably never touch:** 8. **CSV export** — I don't even know what I'd do with this. Who exports CSVs? Accountants? I'm not printing expense reports, I'm just trying to survive co-parenting.

## 4. Willingness to Pay

**Short answer:** Maybe, but not immediately.

**The honest truth:**
I'd start on the Free tier and see if my ex actually uses it. If he logs in twice and ghosts, I'm not paying $5/mo for a fancy spreadsheet I maintain alone.

If we both actually used it for 2-3 months and it reduced our fights, then yes, I'd pay $5/mo. That's less than a single therapy session about money arguments.

**What would make me upgrade to Pro:**

- **Unlimited history** — 3 months is barely enough to see patterns. What if we're arguing about whether he reimbursed me for summer camp last July? I'd need to see back further.
- **Unlimited recurring expenses** — 2 recurring expenses is a joke. We have: school lunch accounts (2 kids), music lessons (2 kids), soccer league fees, gymnastics, after-school care, health insurance premiums. That's 8 right there, and I'm not even counting irregular stuff like annual sports physicals.

The CSV export thing doesn't move the needle for me at all. I don't care about that.

**Is the price right?**
$5/mo feels fair IF:

- My ex also pays, so it's $2.50/month each
- It demonstrably reduces conflict (hard to measure, but I'd know)
- It works reliably and doesn't require constant maintenance

If it's buggy, slow, or requires me to manually fix things, I'd bail. I'm not paying to beta-test someone's app.

**The Free tier problem:**
2 recurring expenses max is so limiting it feels like a trap. It's like "try our app but you can't actually use it for real." I'd need at least 5 recurring expenses to make this work for my actual life. If the Free tier had 5 recurring expenses and 6-month history, I'd probably never upgrade. But 2 feels deliberately crippled.

## 5. Red Flags & Adoption Barriers

**What would stop me from using this:**

1. **Setup friction** — If it takes more than 10 minutes to set up and add my first few expenses, I'm out. I'm busy. I don't have time for complicated onboarding.

2. **Mobile-first or bust** — If this is primarily a desktop app, it's useless to me. I log expenses in the Target parking lot on my phone. I check balances while waiting for soccer practice to end. If I have to open a laptop, this won't become a habit.

3. **The co-parent invitation problem** — How do I invite my ex? What if he ignores the invite? What if he signs up but never logs anything? Does the app just become a personal expense tracker at that point? That's not helpful.

4. **Data privacy fears** — Is this going to sell my data? Show me ads? Get used against me in court somehow? I'm paranoid about this stuff because divorce makes you paranoid.

**Getting my co-parent to use it:**

This is the REAL barrier. Here's the honest scenario:

Me: "Hey, I found this app that could help us track expenses better."
Him: "The Google Sheet is fine."
Me: "You haven't updated it in three weeks."
Him: "I've been busy. I'll update it this weekend."
(He doesn't update it.)

OR:

Him: "Why do you need an app? Are you saying you don't trust me?"
Me: "It's not about trust, it's about having everything in one place."
Him: "Sounds like you're trying to control how I spend money on the kids."

**The emotional/social barriers:**

- Suggesting a new system implies the current system is broken, which implies blame
- It costs money, which means one of us has to pay (or we split it, which requires agreement)
- It requires both people to change their behavior, which is hard
- It creates a permanent record, which some people find threatening
- It might feel like "over-engineering" a simple problem to people who don't experience the conflict

**What MIGHT work:**

- If a mediator or therapist suggested it (neutral third party)
- If it had a "try it solo" mode where I could import our Google Sheet and show him a working balance to prove value
- If there was a "read-only guest access" where he could see the balance without committing to logging expenses yet

## 6. Missing Features

**What I need that isn't here:**

1. **Custody calendar integration** — I want to see which parent had the kids when an expense happened. This would instantly resolve 50% of disputes. "You bought them dinner on my custody night, that's personal, not shared."

2. **Budget caps or spending alerts** — "Hey, we've already spent $400 on activities this month, which is over our usual $300/month average." This would prevent the "$300 trampoline surprise" situation.

3. **Expense approval workflow (optional)** — For big purchases (say, over $100), require the other parent to approve BEFORE spending. This would eliminate the "I already bought it, you have to pay half" trap.

4. **Receipt photos** — Sometimes my ex says "prove you actually paid that much." Being able to attach a photo of the receipt would shut that down. I know this adds complexity, but it's a real need.

5. **Notes/context field** — "Emma's soccer cleats, she grew two sizes" is more useful than just "soccer cleats $45." Context reduces questions.

6. **Notification controls** — I need to know when he logs an expense or questions something, but I don't want to be bombarded. Let me set quiet hours or batch notifications.

7. **Payment request feature** — "You owe me $247, here's a payment request" with one-tap Venmo/Zelle integration. Right now I have to manually calculate, then open Venmo, then type the amount, then send. Friction kills settlement.

8. **Mediation mode** — If we can't resolve a questioned expense, escalate it to our mediator (who has read-only access). This is probably edge-case, but it's a real scenario for high-conflict co-parents.

## 7. The Co-Parent Problem

**How would I convince my co-parent to use this?**

Honestly? I probably couldn't, at least not directly.

Here's what MIGHT work:

**Scenario A: Lead by example**
I use it solo for a month, manually importing our shared expenses. Then I show him: "Look, according to the app, you owe me $312 right now. Here's the breakdown. Do you agree?" If the number is accurate and saves him from doing math, he might opt in.

**Scenario B: Neutral authority**
Our mediator says "You two need to use this app, it's designed for this exact problem." That removes the "you're trying to control me" dynamic.

**Scenario C: Pain point escalation**
After our next big money fight, in the calm aftermath: "This is exhausting for both of us. Can we try something new? I'll pay for the first month."

**What if he refuses?**

Then the app is useless, full stop. You can't track shared expenses if only one person is logging them. I guess I could use it as a personal ledger of what I think he owes me, but that just becomes ammunition for arguments, not resolution.

**Does the app work if only one person uses it?**

No. Not really. Maybe as a record-keeping tool I could show a mediator or lawyer later, but for day-to-day co-parenting, it's a two-person system or nothing.

**This is your biggest problem.** The app might be amazing, but if you can't solve the cold-start problem (getting both parents to commit), it's dead on arrival for most people.

## 8. Emotional Reactions

**"Questioned" vs "Disputed":**

"Questioned" feels softer. "Disputed" sounds legal and adversarial. I appreciate the choice of words. That said, it doesn't change the emotional reality: I'm still saying "I don't believe you" or "I think you're cheating me," which feels awful.

What if instead of "Question Expense" the button said "Ask About This" or "More Info Needed"? That feels collaborative instead of accusatory.

**The balance display:**

This could go either way emotionally.

On good days, seeing "He owes you $183" would feel validating. Like, yes, I'm not crazy, he does owe me money, here's proof.

On bad days, seeing "You owe him $312" would feel stressful and guilt-inducing, especially if money is tight that month.

I think you need to let people hide the balance if they want. Like, "I'll check it when I'm ready, don't show me a big red number every time I open the app."

**Is anything anxiety-inducing?**

Yes, the 7-day auto-resolution for questioned expenses. What if I'm on vacation? What if I just miss the notification? Suddenly his version of events becomes "official" and I lose my ability to object? That feels like a trap.

Also, the 30-day nuclear option to close unresolved items feels extreme. Like, what does "close" mean? Does the expense just disappear? Does it count as approved? This needs clearer language.

**Color-coding status badges:**

If you use red for "questioned" or "disputed," that's going to trigger people. Red = bad = conflict. Maybe use neutral colors (blue, gray, yellow) for intermediate states and green for "resolved."

**Terminology check:**

- "Balance" → neutral, good
- "You owe" / "They owe" → factual, good
- "Questioned" → softer than disputed, good
- "Personal expense" → clear, good
- "Shared expense" → clear, good
- "Settled" → positive, good

Overall the language is pretty good, just watch out for anything that feels accusatory.

## 9. Deal-Breakers

**What single thing, if wrong, would make me delete the app?**

1. **If it's not mobile-first.** If I can't log expenses and check balance from my phone in under 30 seconds, it's useless.

2. **If my co-parent can edit or delete expenses retroactively without my knowledge.** That's a trust nuke. I need immutable history or at least very visible audit logs.

3. **If it's buggy with money math.** If the balance is ever wrong — even once — I lose all confidence. Money math has to be perfect, always.

4. **If customer support is non-existent.** When (not if) something goes wrong, I need a human to help me fast. Chatbots and FAQ pages don't cut it for emotional, high-stakes stuff like co-parent finances.

5. **If it doesn't work offline or has constant syncing issues.** I need to be able to log an expense in a parking lot with spotty service and trust that it'll sync when I get home.

6. **If notifications are broken.** If my ex questions an expense and I don't get notified, and then 7 days later it auto-resolves in his favor, I'll rage-quit.

7. **If the free tier is so limited that I can't actually evaluate whether this works for my life.** 3-month history and 2 recurring expenses is borderline insulting. Give me enough rope to hang myself before demanding $5/mo.

---

## Bottom Line

**Would I use this?** Yes, if my co-parent also committed to it.

**Would I pay for it?** Yes, after 2-3 months of successful use, assuming it reduced conflict and saved time.

**What's the biggest risk?** The co-parent adoption problem. If you can't solve that, the product is DOA.

**What's the biggest opportunity?** The rolling balance + payment recording combo. That's genuinely transformative for people in my situation.

**What needs work?** The Free tier is too restrictive. The questioning workflow is confusing. There's no plan for getting both parents to adopt. And you're missing some key features like custody calendar integration and receipt photos.

**Final honest take:**
This solves a real problem I have. But it also requires me to convince my ex to change his behavior, which might be harder than just living with the current broken system. If you can crack the adoption barrier — maybe through mediators, lawyers, or a killer solo-to-shared migration flow — this could be huge. But right now, I'm not confident I could get my ex on board, and without that, it's just a nice idea.
